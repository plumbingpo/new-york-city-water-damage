Maintenance Tips for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Every member of the household should know where the main water valve is and how it closes.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Replace old supply hoses on washers, toilets and faucets before they burst.
  • Place leak sensors near the water heater, washing machine, dishwasher and under sinks.
  • Being able to find the electrical panel quickly means wet areas can be de-energized without risk.

Warning Signs for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
  • The water keeps running, and the shutoff valve is out of reach or nowhere to be found.
  • The water is from a sewer backup or looks dark and smells bad.
  • Wet carpet, wood flooring or wallboard has stayed saturated longer than a few hours.
  • A leak upstairs is sending water down the walls of the level beneath it.
  • Floor-level power strips, plugged-in appliances or the breaker panel are now wet.
  • Trapped water overhead has made part of the ceiling bulge or sag.
