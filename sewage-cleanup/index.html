A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in New York City, New York City Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
