Ceiling Water Damage Repair in New York City

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Joint tape coming loose, with screws and panel edges telegraphing through
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Older Ceiling Materials

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Electricity Overhead

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Growth on the Hidden Side

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
